Interstate Migration in Australia

Internal migration across Australia refers to the movement of people between states and territories, which affects how the population is distributed throughout the country. This project uses Net Interstate Migration (NIM) data from the Australian Bureau of Statistics to analyse the difference between the number of people moving into and out of each state or territory by age, gender, and financial year. By analysing migration trends, growth regions, and demographic changes, the project aims to make migration data easier to understand and interpret through clear visualisations, providing valuable insights for both decision-makers and individuals considering relocation within Australia.

Net Migration by Region

Between 2017 and 2025, interstate migration patterns across Australia changed significantly. New South Wales experienced consistent migration loss and remained as largest source of departures. Victoria moved from positive net migration before 2020 to substantially more departures during and after the pandemic. Queensland stood out as the leading destination for interstate migrants, attracting more arrivals than departures across most years. Western Australia experienced a gradual increase in arrivals after 2020, while South Australia, Tasmania, the Northern Territory, and the ACT maintained lower but relatively stable migration flows.

Arrivals and Depatures by Time and Region

New South Wales, Victoria, and Queensland are consistently above in both arrivals and departures due to their higher population and stronger labour market. By 2020, around when the COVID-19 pandemic was happening, Interstate Migration Arrivals saw a significant decrease for arrivals for the 3 most populated states. Departures also showed an overall decrease during the pandemic with the exception for Victoria. After the pandemic period, interstate travel remained lower than previous years, likely due to a shift to online working making the need to travel for work less prevalent.

The geographic map highlights a clear shift in population movement across Australia between 2019 to 2025. Eastern states experienced the most amount of interstate travel comapritively to the rest of Australia.

Gender in Interstate Migration

From 2017–2025, interstate migration departures show a very balanced gender distribution across all Australian regions. Males generally recorded slightly higher departure numbers than females, particularly in New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, and Western Australia. However, the gap between genders remained small and consistent throughout the period. Tasmania displayed the most even distribution, with female departures occasionally exceeding male departures. Both genders experienced similar fluctuations over time, including declines around 2021 and gradual recovery afterward, indicating that interstate migration patterns affected males and females in largely comparable ways across regions.

Age in Interstate Migration

Interstate migration arrivals were concentrated among young and working-age adults, particularly those aged 20–39. Queensland attracted the strongest inflows across most age groups, while Victoria and New South Wales also received substantial arrivals. Western Australia showed steady growth in working-age migrants. Arrivals declined progressively with age, with people aged 60 and over representing the smallest share. Departures shows similar distribution of age with Interstate travel.

Between 2017 and 2025, Australia’s population distribution was significantly shaped by patterns of interstate migration, which varied across states and territories. Queensland consistently emerged as the primary destination for interstate migrants, reflecting sustained net inflows driven by factors such as relative housing affordability, employment opportunities, and lifestyle preferences. In contrast, New South Wales and Victoria recorded comparatively higher levels of net population outflow. Nevertheless, both states remained central to national migration dynamics due to their large population bases and their roles as key economic, educational, and employment hubs.

Analysis on Age indicates that interstate migration was predominantly driven by individuals in the young and working-age cohorts, particularly those aged 20–39. This trend reflects the influence of labour market mobility, tertiary education opportunities, and lifestyle transitions associated with early adulthood. In comparison, older age groups exhibited substantially lower levels of interstate mobility, indicating greater residential stability and reduced likelihood of relocation.

Gender focused analysis shows that migration flows were relatively balanced between males and females across all states and throughout the study period, suggesting no significant gender disparity in interstate travel patterns.

Overall, interstate migration played a significant role in reshaping population distribution and contributing to demographic change across Australia during the 2017–2025 period. These trends highlight the emergence of shifting population corridors, particularly towards Queensland and selected regional areas. The findings have important implications for policymakers and planners in relation to housing demand, infrastructure development, labour force planning, and the sustainable management of regional population growth.
